# LumaChart — Competitive Landscape & Positioning

**The market is crowded and the products look alike. LumaChart's edge is not a feature — it's a stance: *evidence-based research built into the record.***
Prepared 2026-07-10 · branch `new-feature`.

> **The one-line thesis.** LumaChart's idea isn't "add AI to the EHR." It's **add evidence-based research to the EHR** — every recommendation carries its citation, every questionnaire is a validated instrument, prevention is USPSTF-graded, and consented data feeds a learning health system. AI is a delivery mechanism; **evidence is the product.**

*Market positions below are approximate, factual industry facts; the landscape shifts constantly. Descriptions are general — no vendor's proprietary material is reproduced.*

## Why "evidence, not AI" is the defensible wedge

Every incumbent is racing to bolt generative AI onto an EHR whose underlying logic is still **documentation-for-billing**. That produces faster billing notes, not better care — and an AI trained to reduce clicks inside a billing-shaped record inherits the record's priorities.

LumaChart inverts the stack:

1. **The record is organized around health** (prevention, healthspan, the Assessment & Plan as the future tense) — so anything built on top optimizes for outcomes, not codes.
2. **Every clinical claim is cited** — real, verified PubMed IDs in the app and the planning document; USPSTF Grade A/B recommendations; validated public-domain instruments (PHQ-9, GAD-7, AUDIT-C). AI here is a *decision-support intervention* with source and logic disclosed (§170.315(b)(11)), not an opaque suggestion.
3. **Consented data becomes knowledge** — the research layer turns routine, structured care into a learning health system that gives back to the population.
4. **The clinician is a first-class user** — Canary, EHR8/WOW8, and the AMA Joy in Medicine workflow treat burnout as a measured, designed-for problem, not an afterthought.

A competitor can copy any single feature. The stance — *the record earns trust because it shows its evidence* — is the harder thing to copy, because it requires building the record around health instead of billing in the first place.

## What we deliberately do **not** do

- **Not** an aesthetics / medical-photography workflow (out of scope; see billing plan).
- **Not** a single-specialty tool with thin code coverage.
- **Not** "AI that writes your note faster" as the headline — that's table stakes, and it's the wrong optimization target.
